# Roadmap

## Guiding Principle

GrowBuddy AI should earn trust with a narrow, reliable daily-planning experience before expanding into multiple coaches and long-term personalization. Dates will be added after scope, team capacity, and validation criteria are agreed.

## Phase 0: Product and Safety Foundation

- Define the target age range and primary family use cases.
- Interview children, parents, educators, and child-safety experts.
- Define parental consent, privacy, retention, and escalation requirements.
- Establish product success metrics and AI evaluation scenarios.
- Prototype the child and parent journeys.

Exit criteria: the team has a validated core problem, a testable MVP scope, and documented safety requirements.

## Phase 1: MVP — Daily Independence

- Create parent and child profiles with family-scoped permissions.
- Let families create tasks, routines, and schedules.
- Provide a child-friendly daily planner and completion flow.
- Add a bounded AI reminder coach with configurable quiet hours.
- Provide a basic parent view of progress and blockers.
- Add audit logging, feedback, and baseline AI evaluations.

Exit criteria: pilot families can plan and complete a week of routines safely, and the team can measure usefulness and reliability.

## Phase 2: Learning Coaches

- Add a reading coach with age-appropriate sessions.
- Add a homework coach focused on guidance rather than answer delivery.
- Introduce goals, rewards, and parent-configurable incentives.
- Improve accessibility, notifications, and onboarding.
- Expand evaluation suites for learning quality and child safety.

Exit criteria: coaching features show measurable engagement or learning value without weakening safety or child autonomy.

## Phase 3: Personalization and Memory

- Add explicit, reviewable long-term memory.
- Adapt plans and reminders to routines and learning preferences.
- Generate parent insights with transparent supporting signals.
- Let families inspect, correct, and delete remembered information.
- Introduce specialized agents only where evaluations show an advantage.

Exit criteria: personalization improves outcomes, and families retain meaningful control over the data that powers it.

## Phase 4: Scale and Expansion

- Migrate production persistence to PostgreSQL when scale requires it.
- Strengthen reliability, monitoring, disaster recovery, and cost controls.
- Explore educator collaboration, localization, and additional learning domains.
- Complete external privacy, security, accessibility, and safety reviews.

## Measures of Success

- Children complete planned routines with less parent prompting.
- Children report feeling more confident and capable, not more monitored.
- Parents find insights useful, understandable, and respectful of privacy.
- AI interactions meet defined safety and quality thresholds.
- Families continue using the product without unhealthy engagement patterns.

## Explicit Non-Goals for the First MVP

- Replacing teachers, tutors, therapists, or parents
- Providing unrestricted web access or open-ended AI chat
- High-stakes academic assessment
- Fully autonomous actions or purchases
- Complex multi-agent infrastructure before one coaching flow is reliable
